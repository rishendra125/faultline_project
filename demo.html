Find out whether to fix the chatbot or fix the checker

  1. A chatbot for a made-up payments app answered 120 questions, and some of its answers are wrong.
  2. You mark the answers good or bad, group the problems, and test an automatic checker (a judge) that tries to spot the bad answers.
  3. At the end, four boxes show whether the problem is in the chatbot or in the checker.
  • Both say bad: a real chatbot problem. Fix the chatbot.
  • Judge too strict: the checker flags answers that are fine. Fix the checker.
  • Judge missed it: the checker lets bad answers through. Fix the checker.
  • Both say good: nothing to fix.

All data is SYNTHETIC (invented). The tour fills in sample answers for you, marked as sample labels, so it is not your own review. Build and test an automatic checker

DEMO JUDGE, simple rules, not AI

An automatic checker (called a judge) reads a question and the bot's answer, then says good or bad. The demo judge follows simple rules, so nothing is paid for and nothing is sent anywhere. A real AI judge can be switched on later. It only ever sees the question and the bot's answer, and its numbers are demo numbers, not a real result.

Step A: Practise (not done)

Try the checker on practice answers. You can do this as often as you like.

Step B: Final test (waiting)

The sealed set is like a final exam. You can open it once for each version of the instructions, and every opening is counted.

Advanced (optional): split, instructions, versions and history

The split

The labeled answers are shared out three ways: examples that go into the instructions, a tuning set to practise on, and a sealed set kept for the final test.

The split uses a fixed seed and keeps the mix of question types and good or bad answers the same in each part. It cannot be changed once the judge has been run. Making it again also saves version 1 again.

The checker's instructions

Made from the fact sheet, your locked groups and a few worked examples. You can edit them. Each saved edit is a new version.

The first draft deliberately leaves out the rule about invented or differing numbers, times and promises, so the fix loop can be shown.

The demo judge also has a few built-in simple checks that do not read these instructions, so this text is not the whole story. A real AI judge follows only its instructions.

The strictness belongs to the version you save. The demo judge follows simple rules and reacts to one sentence: add "Calculated numbers are fine." to the instructions and it stops flagging numbers that are worked out from the question. A real AI judge would respond in its own way.

Live Claude judge (paid, uses your own key)

Model: (fixed, it cannot be changed here)

Off by default. When it is on, the practice and the final test use a real AI judge instead of the demo judge, and every call is paid for with your key. Only the question, the bot answer and the instructions are sent. The instructions contain worked examples with their verdicts, taken only from the examples part. No label of any practice or sealed answer is ever sent, and the sealed set is sent only after you accept the sealed set confirm. The key is kept in memory only, is never saved or exported, and is gone when you close or reload the page.

Before any run you see the number of calls and a rough cost range in two confirm windows. It stops at the first error and never retries on its own. The sealed set opens once per version, whichever judge you use.
